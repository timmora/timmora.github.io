Overview

Aporia builds you a personalized investment portfolio from a 3-step values questionnaire (your goals, risk tolerance through some behavioral scenarios, and any ethical dealbreakers) and keeps updating it as your life changes. Basically every other investing app optimizes for returns. Aporia optimizes for whether you can actually stand behind what you own.

The Problem

Every personal finance app out there optimizes for the same thing: returns. Robinhood basically teaches you to gamble. Vanguard hands you a target-date fund and calls it a day. Even the "responsible investing" ETFs are mostly marketing, their screening criteria are vague, inconsistent, and buried three pages into a prospectus. Nothing let you say "I don't want to own fossil fuel companies" and actually see what that means for your allocation.

The other problem is opacity. Most portfolio tools feel like vending machines, you put inputs in, get outputs out, no explanation in between. I wanted Aporia to feel more like a conversation, where you can actually see the reasoning and understand not just what you own, but why.

Highlights

  • 01

    Values-based onboarding that turns your ethical dealbreakers into real allocation decisions. Exclude fossil fuels and it doesn't just filter a fund, it swaps in a solar ETF at the same weight.

  • 02

    Custom portfolio generation across 12 curated ETFs, with an animated reveal that actually shows why each holding got picked or swapped instead of just dropping a pie chart on you.

  • 03

    Live market data through Yahoo Finance: real-time quotes, historical OHLC, performance charts with an SPY overlay across four time ranges. React Query handles it gracefully if the feed ever goes down.

  • 04

    An institutional intelligence panel that surfaces Q4 2024 SEC 13F filings from Berkshire, ARK, and Vanguard, mapped back to your specific holdings. I haven't seen this in any other consumer investing app.

  • 05

    Life event triggers and a suggestion inbox. New job, baby, home purchase, each one kicks off a tailored review of your allocation. Rebalancing alerts show up when they're actually relevant, not on some fixed schedule.

Under the Hood

The values pipeline runs in three stages. First, the onboarding questionnaire turns your ethical dealbreakers into exclusion rules. "No fossil fuels" means screening out specific sectors and tickers, then swapping in clean energy alternatives at the same weight. Second, the portfolio generator runs an allocation algorithm across those 12 ETFs, weighting for your risk tolerance and time horizon while respecting the exclusions. Third, a 13F parser pulls institutional holdings straight from quarterly SEC filings and cross-references them against what you actually own.

The trickiest part was making rebalancing feel smart instead of mechanical. Triggering a full review every time interest rates twitch would just be noise. So I built a life event model (job change, major purchase, family change) that maps each event to a prioritized set of questions, so suggestions feel earned instead of automatic.

"The institutional intelligence panel started as an afterthought. Showing what Berkshire, ARK, and Vanguard are actually holding this quarter ended up being the most distinctive thing about the product."

Reflection

I built Aporia because I wanted to use it myself. The 13F panel was the biggest surprise. I didn't plan it as a core feature, but once I had the parsing working it felt like the most genuinely new part of the product. I still haven't seen it anywhere else. The SEC already publishes that data every quarter, it just takes someone willing to actually parse it.

Building a values-aligned portfolio tool also changed how I think about financial advice. The algorithm makes suggestions, but your ethics drive the logic, not the other way around. Putting values ahead of the optimizer's objective function is basically the whole product philosophy.
