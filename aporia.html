Overview

Aporia is a personal finance app that generates customized investment portfolios from a 3-step values questionnaire — goals, risk tolerance via behavioral scenarios, and ethical dealbreakers — and keeps them updated with intelligent suggestions as your life changes. Existing investing tools optimize for returns. Aporia optimizes for alignment.

The Problem

Every personal finance app on the market optimizes for the same thing: returns. Robinhood teaches you to gamble. Vanguard gives you target-date funds. Even the "responsible investing" ETFs are mostly marketing — their screening criteria are opaque, inconsistently applied, and buried in prospectus footnotes. There was no tool that let someone say "I don't want to own fossil fuel companies" and actually understand what that meant at the allocation level.

The other problem was opacity. Most portfolio tools feel like vending machines: inputs in, outputs out, no explanation. I wanted Aporia to work more like a conversation — one where the reasoning is visible and the user understands not just what they own, but why.

Highlights

  • 01

    Values-based onboarding that translates ethical dealbreakers into actual allocation decisions — a fossil fuel exclusion doesn't just filter a fund, it triggers a solar ETF swap at the allocation level.

  • 02

    Custom portfolio generation across 12 curated ETFs with an animated reveal that shows why each holding was selected or swapped, making the logic transparent rather than opaque.

  • 03

    Live market data via Yahoo Finance — real-time quotes, historical OHLC, and performance charts with SPY benchmark overlay across four time ranges. React Query handles stale states gracefully when the feed is unavailable.

  • 04

    Institutional intelligence panel surfacing Q4 2024 SEC 13F filings from Berkshire, ARK, and Vanguard — mapped back to the user's specific holdings. A feature I haven't seen in any consumer investing tool.

  • 05

    Life event triggers and a suggestion inbox: new job, baby, home purchase each generate a tailored review of the current allocation. Rebalancing alerts surface when relevant, not on a fixed schedule.

Under the Hood

The values pipeline works in three stages. First, the onboarding questionnaire converts ethical dealbreakers into exclusion rules — "no fossil fuels" maps to screening out specific sectors and tickers, then replacing them with clean energy alternatives at the same allocation weight. Second, the portfolio generator runs an allocation algorithm across 12 curated ETFs, weighting for risk tolerance and time horizon while enforcing the exclusion rules. Third, the 13F parser pulls institutional holdings from quarterly SEC filings and cross-references them against the user's specific holdings.

The trickiest part was making rebalancing feel smart rather than mechanical. Triggering a full portfolio review every time interest rates shift would be noise. I built a life event model — job change, major purchase, family change — that maps each event type to a prioritized set of questions, so rebalancing suggestions feel earned and relevant rather than automatic.

"The institutional intelligence panel — surfacing what Berkshire, ARK, and Vanguard are actually holding this quarter — was an afterthought that became the product's most distinctive feature."

Reflection

I built Aporia because I wanted to use it. The 13F intelligence panel was the most unexpected outcome — I didn't plan it as a core feature, but once I had the parsing working, it felt like the most genuinely novel piece of the product. I haven't seen it in any consumer investing tool. The SEC publishes that data quarterly; it just takes someone willing to parse it.

Building a values-aligned portfolio tool also made me think differently about what financial advice means. The algorithms make suggestions, but the user's ethics drive the logic. That inversion — putting values ahead of the optimizer's objective function — is the whole product philosophy.
